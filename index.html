Sources & Method

Season-long PPR points blend two independently-modeled sources rather than one: Razzball (Rudy Gamble's model) publishes a full-board rest-of-season projection covering nearly every player on this sheet, so it's used as the primary number wherever it exists; FantasyPros' consensus draft-season model only auto-exposes its top ~10 backs and ~10 receivers, so it fills in nothing Razzball doesn't already cover and serves as a cross-check where both exist. The two disagreed by more than 15 points on a handful of players (a real, sourced disagreement, not an error) — those are flagged in the Draft Note column rather than silently picking one. A player neither source models numerically (mostly deep bench/practice-squad-adjacent names) stays blank rather than getting an invented figure. Week 1 points use FantasyPros' actual Week 1 projection for the players it publishes one for; every other player's Week 1 number is this document's own season ÷ 17 average, clearly marked with a "~" — nobody publishes individually-modeled Week 1 stat lines for the full league, so that average is the honest option rather than a guess dressed up as a real projection. Attempts/targets volume, where shown, comes from FantasyPros' exposed top tier only. Depth charts and role calls are cross-checked against team-released unofficial depth charts, beat-reporter reporting, and multiple fantasy outlets; committee/injury situations note disagreement explicitly. Workload splits marked "est." are this document's own estimate from the reporting below, not a stat a team publishes — treat them as directional, not exact. 2025 Season numbers (shown on each player's card) are real, final Pro-Football-Reference regular-season stat lines, verified individually per player — not projections. PPR points there are computed from the raw stat line (0.1/rush yd, 6/rush TD, 1/reception, 0.1/rec yd, 6/rec TD), not PFR's own scoring column, so they match this sheet's full-PPR format exactly. A player tagged "Rookie" was in college during the 2025 season and has no NFL stat line yet. ADP and draft round reflect FantasyFootballCalculator's live 10-team PPR mock-draft data, not ESPN's or Yahoo's own numbers — those platforms only expose ADP inside their logged-in draft tools, with no public page to pull from, so FFC's real mock-draft sample (thousands of actual 10-team PPR drafts, most recent week) is the honest public substitute rather than a guess at what those sites would show. Round is simply ADP ÷ 10, rounded up, for a 10-team league specifically — it will not match a 12- or 14-team league's rounds.
